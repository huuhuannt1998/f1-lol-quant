How it works

LoL. Every completed pro match since 2024 updates an Elo rating, one step per game. New teams start at their league's average. Ratings shrink part-way toward the league mean each new year, and international games count extra because they are the only evidence of how regions compare. The game win probability becomes a series probability for Bo1, Bo3 or Bo5. A logistic regression and a LightGBM model then try to improve on Elo using recent form, rest days and, when available, rolling in-game stats from Oracle's Elixir. Whichever model scored best in the walk-forward backtest makes the published forecast.

F1. Each driver gets a score from recent finishing form, constructor form, qualifying form, reliability, circuit history and, after qualifying, grid position and gap to pole. Scores become win probabilities with a softmax inside each race, so they always sum to 100%. Podium and points chances come from simulating 20,000 finishing orders (Plackett–Luce).

Honesty rules. Features only ever use information from before the event. Backtests are walk-forward: each month or race is predicted by a model fitted only on earlier data. The Track record tab uses only forecasts written to the append-only ledger before each start, scored against the Kalshi price at that same moment.

Paper trades are hypothetical: one $1 contract when the model beats the price by at least 5¢ after an estimated fee. Real fills, liquidity and fees differ. This is a personal learning project, not financial advice.
